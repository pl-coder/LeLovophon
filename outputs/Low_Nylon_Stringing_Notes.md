# Low nylon string bed — stringing and tension adjustment

This version lowers all six strings by 18 mm, to a nominal 18 mm above the soundboard. The whole arm bank remains centred along the instrument. The arm pivots are lowered with the strings, while the cam follower pivots remain at their original height. New dropped bearing lugs and offset step-up belts connect the two. Arm radii, speaking lengths, 24 mm bank pitch and harmonic timing ratios are retained. The original wider swing amplitudes are retained.

The full string path is: left tied anchor → rounded left end guide → common left saddle → movable third bridge → speaking length → common right saddle → rounded right end guide → external tie-on tensioning shuttle. All six strings use nominal 0.8 mm unwound nylon in this geometric study. The material grade, diameter, pitch register, operating tension and bend life are not yet specified for manufacture.

The movable third bridges keep the speaking lengths at 330, 293⅓, 264, 247½, 220 and 198 mm. In the ideal string model, identical string material, diameter and tension produce the intended inverse length / pitch ratios. Individual tension adjustment is still needed in practice. Moving a bridge also changes string deflection and can change tension; retune after moving one.

Each right-hand tensioner has a tie-on shuttle, an M4 × 0.7 screw, and approximately 20 mm of guided travel. Pull the string approximately taut by hand and tie it to the shuttle near the upper end of the adjustment range, then turn the screw to pull the shuttle down. The bottom shelf reacts screw thrust, and the end plate attaches to the reinforced body end. Initial nylon stretch may require retying to restore adjustment travel. Screw threads are modelled as cylinders; thread form, thrust washers, captive retention, guide tolerances and structural fastening details need selection before manufacture. These are screw-pull tensioners, not geared winding tuners.

End guides have a nominal 6 mm string-path radius. Their finish, groove profile, wear resistance and attachment require prototype testing with the chosen nylon. No sharp bare body edge should contact a tensioned string. Knot geometry in the model is illustrative, not a tested knot specification. The 3 mm soundboard is not intended to carry end tension unaided: the reinforced end blocks and their attachments must carry the resulting loads.

Traditional koto stringing provides the design reference: strings pass through body holes and are tied off, with spare length retained; movable bridges adjust pitch. Our external hand-screw adjustment is an adaptation for easier servicing.

This is a geometric prototype. Drive power, spring contact force, compliant pick release, nylon creep, structural strength, fatigue and acoustic performance remain unverified. The animation, if used, prescribes motion and does not simulate these effects.

The current assembled envelope, including the external tensioners, is 394 × 200 × 179 mm. The estimated model material mass is approximately 2.16 kg, excluding unmodelled clockwork, fastening hardware, finishes and case. All 250 modelled part shapes are valid; the file includes saved visibility settings. The sampled clearance report covers 96 positions per voice and reports no unintended moving-to-fixed intersections; exact straight-down poses confirm all six intended pick/string intersections. These checks do not validate dynamic plucking. Third-bridge seating, break angle and preload require acoustic prototype adjustment.
